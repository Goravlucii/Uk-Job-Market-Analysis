THE CENTRAL FINDING

The Contradiction That Drove This Research

📋 What Official Data Says

  • Unemployment: 4.4% — near historic lows
  • Open vacancies: 893,000 roles advertised
  • Nominal wages growing 5%+ year-on-year
  • GDP returned to positive growth in 2024

🎓 What Graduates Report

  • 200–500 applications before a single offer
  • Interview-to-offer conversion: <3%
  • 53.2% of 2024 graduates not in graduate employment 6 months post-graduation
  • Top rejection reason: "insufficient experience" (on entry-level roles)
Conclusion: The headline statistics mask a fragmented labour market. The UK job market has recovered at the top — but not at the graduate entry level. The experience paradox, ATS filtering, macroeconomic suppression, and geographic concentration are structural barriers that low unemployment figures do not capture.

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

Five Structural Barriers to Graduate Employment

01

Skills Mismatch

74% of postings require SQL. 82% require Excel. Yet these skills are rarely core to university Business degrees. The gap between what employers need and what graduates learn is widening.

SQL appears in 74% of analyst postings
02

The Experience Paradox

62.8% of roles labelled "entry level" or "graduate" require 1+ years of prior experience. This creates a structural barrier for first-time applicants that no amount of applications overcomes.

62.8% of 'entry-level' roles need experience
03

ATS Filtering

Applicant Tracking Systems screen CVs automatically. Industry research suggests 70%+ of applications are rejected before human review — often due to keyword mismatches rather than genuine unsuitability.

~70% rejected before human review (ISE)
04

Macroeconomic Suppression

The BoE raised rates from 0.1% to 5.25% (2021–2023). Correlation analysis shows a -0.71 Pearson r between base rate and vacancy volume at 6-month lag — confirming rate hikes depressed hiring.

Pearson r = -0.71 (rate vs vacancies, 6-month lag)
05

Geographic Concentration

86.2% of graduate analyst postings are in London, which contains only 13% of the UK population. This creates an extreme geographic bottleneck for graduates outside the capital.

86.2% of postings are in London
