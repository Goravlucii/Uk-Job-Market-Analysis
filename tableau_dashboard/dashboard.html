Central Finding: The UK labour market has recovered at the headline level but a structural gap has opened at the graduate entry level. Vacancies are at their lowest since 2012, while the number of applications per graduate vacancy hit a record 140 in 2025 (up from 38 in 2002/03). The April 2025 NI rise caused an estimated 167,000 job losses. Five economic shocks since 2020 have combined to create a market that looks healthy in aggregate but is acutely competitive for new entrants.

The Hidden Crisis: The headline unemployment figure (4.9%) understates the true scale of labour market weakness. The economically inactive population has risen by 1.14 million since 2019 — people who have left the labour market entirely and are no longer counted as unemployed. Of these, 2.8m are long-term sick, driven by post-COVID mental health conditions and NHS waiting lists.

Python Pearson Statistical Test: Correlation between BoE base rate and vacancy volume at a 6-month lag: r = -0.71, p = 0.000031. This is statistically significant at the 99.99% confidence level. Every 1 percentage point rise in the base rate preceded a fall of approximately 47,000 vacancies six months later. The mechanism: higher rates raise company borrowing costs, compress margins, and trigger headcount freezes before redundancies.

The Graduate Paradox: 88% of graduates are in work or study within 15 months, but only 53% are in roles classified as graduate-level. This means 35% are underemployed — working below their qualification level while paying back student loans averaging £45,000. The experience paradox (62.8% of ‘entry-level’ roles requiring experience) is the single biggest structural barrier identified in this analysis.
